## Decisions (owner, 2026-10-23)

### D1. Interface languages

Seven interface languages are added: Japanese (`ja`), Korean (`ko`),
Spanish (`es`; one translation for Mexico and Spain), Brazilian Portuguese
(`pt-BR`), Turkish (`tr`), Arabic (`ar`, right to left) and German (`de`).
English stays the default and the fallback.

India gets no interface language of its own. The English store text is
tuned for IELTS instead; which App Store Connect localization India's
store shows is checked in Phase 4.

**Why:** the chosen markets combine low-to-mid English levels, a large
iPhone share and high App Store spending. China, Hong Kong and Russia are
out of scope because they are regions Anthropic does not support.

### D2. Questions and AI explanations stay in English

The questions and every AI explanation (Daily Test explanations, practice
feedback) stay in English in every interface language.

**Why:** the target level is B1–C1; the quality of AI content in seven
languages cannot be verified; cost. After the release, practice completion
rate is compared by language and this decision is reviewed. The language
comes from the `ui_language` user property (B3; added 2026-10-23).

**Seen on the device (owner, 2026-11-02, Turkish pass):** the instructions
generated with each question ("Fill in the blank with the correct form of
the verb …") stay English in a translated interface. That is D2 as
decided: they are part of the question, not interface text.

### D3. Language-neutral proxy prompts

The proxy prompts stop assuming a Turkish learner. "Turkish learners",
"Turkish native speaker" and "Turkish keyboard" become "B1–C1 learners
whose first language is not English", and the keyboard tolerance becomes
keyboard-independent (accents, autocorrect, spelling).

Prompts affected (`proxy/src/anthropic.ts` as of `1126528`): practice
generation (`GENERATION_SYSTEM_PROMPT`), scoring
(`SCORING_SYSTEM_PROMPT`, the "Turkish keyboard" paragraph), the shared
Daily Test generator (`SHARED_DAILY_TEST_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2`) and its checker
(`SHARED_CHECK_SYSTEM_PROMPT`).

**Keyboard folding (added 2026-10-23, owner):** D3 also covers the answer
folding outside the prompts. `foldKeyboardVariants`
(`lib/utils/answer_matching.dart`) and its proxy copy
(`proxy/src/shared_daily_test.ts`) fold Turkish-keyboard letters today;
both are generalized to the seven languages' keyboards: full-width Latin
letters and spaces, accented letters, smart quotes and apostrophes. The
client and the proxy use the same rule. The approach is proposed in
Batch 0.

**Why:** with seven interface languages the learner's first language is no
longer Turkish. **Condition:** the Daily Test prompt reaches every live
user at once (one shared set per date), so its quality is measured before
it is deployed.

### D4. Practice model measurement

With D3's prompts, `claude-sonnet-4-6` (the practice model today,
`proxy/src/anthropic.ts:24`) and `claude-sonnet-5-5` are measured on the
same test set. For scoring, `low` and `medium` effort are tried too. The
report gives, as a separate line, the difference in counting a user's
answer as right or wrong.

**Why:** the move to a language-neutral prompt is a natural point to
re-measure the model. The choice is the owner's; the expectation is a
move to `claude-sonnet-5-5`.

### D5. Widget

One small home-screen widget (WidgetKit). Content: the avatar, this
month's Monthly Climb step ("12 / 31"), and whether today's Daily Test is
done. No streak: Monthly Climb does not punish a missed day. The widget's
text is translated into the seven languages. Later versions add more.

**Why:** a reminder on the home screen without a streak's pressure,
consistent with Monthly Climb's design.

### D6. Translation method

Translations are made by AI only, with no native-speaker review (owner
decision). ~~Feature names stay in English in every language: Daily Test,
Topic Practice, Review, Monthly Climb, Premium.~~ **Changed (owner,
2026-11-02, after the Turkish device pass):** the feature names are
**translated** in every language, the same word every time (Turkish:
Günlük Test, Konu Pratiği, Aylık Tırmanış, Tekrar); only the brand stays,
GrammarLens and Premium. **Why:** the names are functions, not brands;
"Review" or "Daily Test" carry no meaning for a learner in the other six
languages. **The topic titles stay English** in every language (Gerund vs.
Infinitive, Modal Verbs, …): they name English grammar points and match
the hint cards and the questions; their descriptions are translated
(reverses B3's Batch 0 §8 Q2 on the titles).

For the paywall, the AI permission screen and onboarding's "Your data &
AI" text, a back-translation check: in a separate step, the translation is
translated back into English and compared with the original.

The roadmap marks the translations "AI translation, no native review".

**Why:** the owner's decision on cost and speed; the back-translation
check covers the texts where a wrong meaning has legal or App Review
weight (prices and trials, what is sent to the AI service).

### D7. P14 now, deployed right after 1.3.0 is live

P14 (other accepted answers on the live shared sets, server side) is
developed now and deployed right after 1.3.0 is live. **Rule:** no proxy
deploy while 1.3.0 is in App Store review.

**Why:** P14 is server side and independent of an app release (roadmap,
"Moved out of 1.1.0"); the review rule keeps the reviewed build's backend
unchanged while it is being tested.

### D8. Partial answers on multi-blank questions, and per-blank "You wrote"

- **Submit/Next rule, Practice and Daily Test alike:** enabled when **at
  least one** box is filled (typed or "—"). This **replaces Q3 of
  2026-10-12** ("every box typed or set to no-article";
  [`question-screen-plan.md`](question-screen-plan.md)). Zero boxes filled
  stays disabled; Skip is the way out. Empty boxes are sent as unanswered:
  `assembleBlanks` keeps an empty box's `____` in the sentence, the
  intended wire format, so the scorer sees which blanks were left.
  **Why:** an empty box is itself an answer (the learner did not know it);
  forcing the learner to type something they do not know produces worse
  data than a blank.
- **Verified in Batch 0, not changed:** the scorer path sends the raw
  string and the proxy accepts it (`validation.ts`, empty allowed, no
  look at underscores); the scoring prompt special-cases only an *empty*
  answer. The Daily Test's local matcher compares normalised strings for
  equality, so a leftover `____` is `fallback` (wrong), never correct,
  never a crash; in practice every Daily Test source has exactly one
  blank, so one box.
- **Guard (client):** when the wire answer still matches the blank
  pattern, `isCorrect` is forced false whatever the model says. The
  model's error type is kept as returned; when it gave none, no error
  entry is saved and the card reads "Needs work" with the correction.
  **Why:** a "Correct" card over a visibly empty box contradicts the rule
  above; the check is pure and goes when structured feedback lands.
- **Storage:** `error_entries.user_blanks TEXT`, nullable, schema v24 via
  `_addColumnIfMissing`: a JSON list in blank order, each entry the typed
  text, a no-article flag, or an empty text for a box left blank. Null for
  single-box answers and every old row, which render as today. **Rejected:**
  recovering the boxes by aligning the assembled sentence with the prompt
  (the stored prompt is the full text while assembly ran on the context,
  the instruction or both; a no-article box removes a space and may
  capitalise the next word; a one-space segment makes the match
  ambiguous).
- **Results screen:** the per-blank snapshot is taken at Submit from the
  drafts and passed with the answers map; no storage needed there.
- **Display (`MistakeBreakdown`):** with two or more per-blank entries,
  "YOU WROTE" shows one row per blank: a 3 pt bar in
  `blankColors(brightness)[i % 4]`, then the typed text, "—" for
  no-article, or "Left blank" in `onSurfaceVariant`. The prompt above the
  rows colours its blanks with the question card's span builder, so a
  row's colour points at its blank. Single box unchanged; "CORRECTED"
  stays the full corrected sentence.

### D9. Long AI feedback formatted for reading

Client-side formatting only; what the proxy returns is unchanged. A
stopgap until the scorer returns structured per-blank feedback (planned
with the D3/D4 measurement), so the formatter is a pure function over a
string (`lib/utils/feedback_format.dart`) and one widget (`FeedbackText`),
easy to delete; stored plain-text feedback renders through the same path.

- **Sites:** exactly two. `MistakeBreakdown`'s explanation (the Results
  cards, the weak-spot detail's "Recent mistakes" cards and the Daily Test
  result cards) and the weak-spot detail's "Saved feedback" panel. The
  Review list card's two-line excerpt stays plain.
- **Split:** a sentence ends at `.`, `!` or `?` outside parentheses,
  optionally followed by closing quotes or brackets, then whitespace, then
  a capital letter or an opening quote; not after e.g., i.e., etc. or vs.
  Three or more sentences → one paragraph per sentence; one or two → a
  single paragraph.
- **Closing paragraph:** only with **four** or more sentences, the last
  sentence gets a thin `outlineVariant` rule above it; no label. With
  exactly three sentences, three plain paragraphs and no rule (a
  three-blank feedback has no closing sentence). **Why no label:** "the
  last sentence is the lesson" is a guess, and a wrong rule costs less
  than a wrong "In short".
- **Emphasis:** single- and double-quoted spans, quotes included. A quote
  opens at the start or after whitespace, a bracket, a double quote or a
  dash; it closes at the first matching quote followed by the end,
  whitespace or punctuation (no nesting); it must close within the same
  sentence and within 80 characters, else the text stays plain. An
  apostrophe preceded by a letter never opens; a closing-shaped quote with
  nothing open is ignored. Known limit: a possessive inside a quoted
  phrase closes it early. Style: `withWeight(FontWeight.w700)` in the
  surrounding colour; no accent colour (`secondary` is the link colour and
  would read as tappable; the brand orange fails contrast on the light
  card).
- **On the real sample** (the owner's device, 2026-10-31, four sentences):
  three body paragraphs, one per blank or pair of blanks, and the closing
  "Three of the four blanks…" sentence under the rule; eleven quoted
  examples emphasised.
- **Device check (owner, iPhone 14 Plus, release, 2026-10-31):**
  paragraphs, the closing rule and the emphasis render in light and dark,
  but weight alone does not separate the quotes from the body on the dark
  surface. Replaced by D10's chip.

### D10. Word-level difference between "You wrote" and "Corrected"; the quote chip

**Why:** the learner had to scan two long sentences to find what changed.
B8's rows cover only new multi-blank records; the comparison also works on
old records and on sentence-writing and error-correction questions, which
have no blanks. Purely client side over the stored strings; no schema.

- **Tokens:** split on whitespace. Compare key = curly quotes straightened,
  lowercased, leading quotes and brackets stripped, trailing quotes,
  brackets and sentence punctuation stripped; inner apostrophes kept; a
  token without a letter or digit (a lone dash) is neutral. The rendered
  text keeps the original spelling and punctuation; only a token's core is
  painted.
- **Diff:** longest common subsequence over the token lists, dynamic
  programming with a backtrack, computed in `build`. O(n·m) time and
  memory: two 60-word sentences are 3,721 cells.
- **Assembled mode** when the stored prompt contains a blank and an
  in-order alignment of the answer against the prompt matches at least
  80 % of the non-blank tokens of the prompt's **blank-bearing lines**
  (B10.1, 2026-10-31: the answer is assembled from the text that holds the
  blanks, `blankSourceText`, so a context line without a blank is never in
  it; measured over the whole stored prompt, the device's case 2 record
  matched 69 % and fell to simple mode, painting "Complete these two
  sentences", "(hold)" and "and"; over its instruction line it matches
  100 %): prompt text is never painted in
  "You wrote"; parenthesised prompt hints (`(change)`) are left out of the
  diff against the correction, since a hint would otherwise steal the
  match from the corrected word; the prompt alignment keeps parentheses in
  its key so a hint can only match itself. Otherwise **simple mode**: every
  answer token is the learner's (error correction, sentence writing, one
  word).
- **Noise guard:** no highlighting when the changed share exceeds 40 % on
  either side and the longer side has at least six words; share = painted
  over compared words, the sentence region only in assembled mode. The
  real cases sit at 13–14 %, a mild rewrite at 23 %, a heavy rewrite at
  44 %; the six-word minimum keeps one-word answers highlighted. Without a
  prompt: simple mode, and nothing is painted when the answer has at least
  1.5× the correction's words and at least six (case 1 without its prompt
  is exactly 1.5×: 54 to 36 words; the record said "more than", the code
  says "at least" for that reason). One empty side paints nothing.
- **Results on the device cases:** case 1 paints changing · to · join ·
  staying against to · change · joining · to · stay; "to procrastinate",
  the hints and the instruction prefix stay plain. Case 2 paints to · hold
  · staying against holding · to · stay; the "and" between the sentences
  stays plain. Case 2 with the first box left blank paints only "staying"
  and all three corrected words.
- **Colours, measured** (text on highlight / highlight on its box; the
  "You wrote" box is `surface`, the "Corrected" box `correctBackground`):
  - "You wrote": fill `incorrectBackground`, text `onIncorrectBackground`
    (`#F8E1E0` / `#5C1210` light, `#3A1D1D` / `#F4B8B4` dark): 10.9 / 1.25
    light, 9.0 / 1.23 dark. The tint is faint against the box, so a 1.5 pt
    underline in `onIncorrectBackground` is the shape cue. **B10.2 (owner,
    2026-10-31):** the underline removed, it collided with descenders and
    made the misspelt word harder to read; the fill darkened a step
    instead, `incorrectBackground` with 15 % of `onIncorrectBackground`
    over it: `#E1C2C1` light (text 8.2:1, fill on box 1.66:1), `#563434`
    dark (text 6.4:1, fill on box 1.73:1).
  - "Corrected": the inverse solid pill, fill `onCorrectBackground`, text
    `correctBackground` (`#1E4620` / `#DCEEDF` light, `#B7E4C1` /
    `#1B3320` dark): 8.9 / 8.9 light, 9.7 / 9.7 dark. **Rejected:** a tint
    of `onCorrect` at 24 % over the box (5.9 / 1.5 light, 5.2 / 1.9 dark).
- **Coexistence with B8:** on a new multi-blank record the rows stay and
  only "Corrected" is painted; the assembled `userAnswer` is still what is
  compared. Old records without `user_blanks`: both boxes painted.
- **Quote chip (replaces B9's weight-only emphasis):** translucent
  `onSurface` fill, 12 % light and 14 % dark, radius 4, 3 pt horizontal
  padding, weight 600, text colour unchanged; applied in `FeedbackText`
  only. Measured: light `#E4E0DA` on the card, text 13.1:1, chip 1.27:1 on
  the card and on the panel; dark `#414143`, text 8.7:1, chip 1.50 / 1.49.
  **Rejected:** `surfaceContainerHighest` as the fill (1.0:1 on the "Saved
  feedback" panel, which is that colour); orange text (`#C2570C`, 4.37:1
  on the light card, under AA, and blank 1's colour on the same cards).
  The boxes never pass through the formatter, so no text carries both a
  chip and a pill.
- **Inline rendering:** `WidgetSpan` for chips and pills, so radius and
  padding render; a chip or pill over several words is one `WidgetSpan`
  per word (outer corners rounded, inner edges square), so a long quoted
  phrase wraps like text. **B10.1 (2026-10-31):** every piece but the last
  carries its trailing space inside the mark, so the fill and the "You
  wrote" underline run on across the gap and a phrase reads as one
  continuous strip (on the device the pieces showed as separate boxes, one
  paragraph as about 25 chip pieces); consecutive painted words in a box
  form one phrase. **B10.2:** the horizontal padding sits on a strip's
  outer edges only; an inner edge already has the space carried inside the
  piece, and padding there widened the word gaps ("to  hold"). Each box
  and each paragraph carries a semantics label of its plain text. Verified
  in tests: no overflow at 320 pt and the Large text size on the real case
  1 feedback; a three-word phrase's pieces touch; the gap inside a strip is
  one space of its style, within a pixel.
- **Device check (owner, iPhone 14 Plus, light and dark, Large text,
  2026-10-31): B10, B10.1 and B10.2 passed.** The two-line record paints
  only the learner's words; multi-word strips are continuous; the darker
  "You wrote" fill without the underline reads well. B8's rows on a fresh
  multi-blank Practice remain unseen (the owner's free quota was used; he
  checks on 2026-11-01).

### D11. Structured scoring feedback

The scorer returns, per item, one entry per blank (one entry for a
single-answer item) with the correct form, whether the learner's answer is
accepted and a one-sentence note, plus a short overall `summary` (two
sentences at most). The learner's per-blank text is **not** in the
response: the client pairs the entries with B8's boxes by index, so the
request does not change. **Why:** B9's formatting and B10's word
difference are stopgaps over one long paragraph; per-blank data makes the
card exact and the "Correct" verdict consistent with the blank guard (B8).
**Constraints:** the new fields are additive and optional; `explanation`
stays today's full text for live clients (revisited when the legacy route
goes); the client keeps accepting today's shape (old proxy, old stored
records) with the B9/B10 widgets as the fallback renderer; storage is one
nullable column, schema v25. The client half ships in the 1.4.0 build; the
proxy half deploys with D3 after the measurement.

**Seen on the device (owner, 2026-11-02):** on a multi-blank record the
